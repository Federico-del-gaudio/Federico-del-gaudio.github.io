STILL HEAR

Team Project • 16 People

Role: Game & Technical Designer

About the Game

Still Hear is a third-person puzzle-stealth adventure following Keepo, one of the last Sound Keepers, and Kee, his living precious heart, through a world where silence has swallowed everything.

Combine their unique abilities to solve puzzles, outsmart the terrifying Sound Eaters, and restore the forgotten harmony.

This is a story-driven puzzle-stealth experience designed for players who crave immersive, indie narratives that blend engaging puzzle and stealth sections.

Instead of relying on switches and keys, players solve puzzles by mastering the behavior of sound through resonance, timing, and acoustic interactions. Every solution is a symphony of stealth and strategy.

Implementation Insights — Puzzles

Sound-Based Puzzle Mechanics

In Still Hear, puzzles are not about keys and switches, they're about mastering sound behavior. I prototyped puzzles that leverage resonance, timing, and acoustic interactions to create meaningful challenges.

Working closely with programmers, I used internal tools to tune puzzle parameters adjusting resonance ranges, timing windows, and interaction feedback to ensure puzzles felt intuitive yet rewarding.

The goal was to make every puzzle feel like a natural extension of the game's stealth and sound mechanics, reinforcing the narrative theme of a world where silence has swallowed everything.

Balancing Abilities & Behaviors

I worked extensively on tuning player abilities and enemy behaviors to create a balanced and engaging experience. This involved adjusting movement speeds, ability cooldowns, detection ranges, and reaction times.

Working side-by-side with programmers, I helped define how mechanics should feel and how enemies should react to player actions. This close collaboration allowed us to iteratively refine the tuning until the gameplay felt just right.

Data-driven design was key, all parameters were exposed through designer-friendly tools, allowing for rapid iteration based on playtest feedback.

Implementation Insights — Feedback & VFX

Polishing Player & Enemy Feedback

Game feedback is vital for immersion. I focused on refining visual, audio, and interface feedback for both player actions and enemy interactions.

I worked with programmers to define and tune VFX parameters from hit effects to stealth indicators ensuring each interaction felt responsive and satisfying.

For audio implementation, I used programmer-provided tools to set up and tune sound cues that reinforce the game's atmospheric and stealth mechanics, making every step and whisper matter.

Closing Thoughts

This was one of my first group projects reviewed and presented to publishers, and I grew a lot from it, not only technically, but also in soft skills.

Working alongside a programmer and building a GDD clear enough for the rest of the team to follow was not an easy challenge, but I'm satisfied with the final result.

During this project I truly understood the importance of playtesting. We ran four playtests, and each time we either iterated on mechanics, reworked them completely, or removed them entirely.
