About the Game

A relentless PS1-style bullet hell set in a flooded carnival of screaming masks. Every shot you fire fuels a distorted breakcore soundtrack that surges in volume with your aggression. Survive the nightmare. Dance until you drown in the noise.

In One Night in Venice, your only goal is to shoot and survive waves of ruthless enemies who want nothing but your end. Keep your eyes sharp and your reflexes faster—if they catch you, you'll have to start it all over AGAIN. Push yourself to beat your personal record with every run. But above all else: don't stop running.

Implementation Insights - Shooting System

Key Learnings from Shooting System Implementation

Developing the hold-to-fire shooting mechanic taught me valuable lessons about projectile management and performance optimization in high-intensity bullet hell scenarios. The system needed to handle dozens of simultaneous projectiles while maintaining smooth framerates.

The most significant insight was implementing object pooling for projectiles. Instead of spawning and destroying thousands of bullets, I created a reusable pool system that dramatically improved performance during intense combat sequences.

I also learned the importance of visual feedback loops—the shooting system is directly tied to the breakcore soundtrack, with fire rate and projectile density affecting audio intensity. This creates a powerful feedback loop where player aggression directly shapes the experience.

Technical Challenge

The hold-to-fire system required careful input buffering to prevent frame-perfect issues while maintaining responsive feel. I implemented a cooldown system with visual indicators for weapon readiness.

Implementation Insights - Enemy Progressive Spawner

Click image to view larger - Progressive spawner system with difficulty scaling based on survival time.

Technical Challenge

Balancing difficulty progression was crucial. The spawner uses a time-based difficulty curve that increases enemy count, speed, and aggressiveness while ensuring the player always has a fighting chance.

Progressive Difficulty Design

The enemy progressive spawner was designed to create escalating tension throughout each run. I implemented a modular system where spawn parameters evolve based on survival time and player performance.

One key insight was creating enemy variety through parameter modification rather than separate enemy types. By adjusting speed, fire rate, and movement patterns over time, the same enemy base feels progressively more threatening.

The spawner also tracks player position and creates tactical pressure points, forcing the player to constantly move and reposition—reinforcing the core mantra: don't stop running.

Implementation Insights - Parry & Audio Integration

Parry Mechanic & Dynamic Audio

The parry mechanic required precise timing windows and clear visual feedback. I implemented a 12-frame parry window with successful parries granting brief invulnerability and pushing back surrounding enemies.

For the breakcore soundtrack integration, I created an audio intensity system that monitors combat metrics: shots fired, enemies near, and time since last parry. This drives real-time DSP effects including bitcrushing, tempo modulation, and volume layering.

The most rewarding insight was how gameplay and audio became inseparable—players learn to read combat intensity through audio cues, and their aggressive play literally shapes the music. The soundtrack isn't just background; it's gameplay feedback.
