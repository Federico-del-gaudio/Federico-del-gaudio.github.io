One Night in Venice

Solo project

Objective: Shooting mechanic, Enemy spawner & power ups

About the Game

A relentless PS1-style bullet hell set in a flooded carnival of screaming masks. Every shot you fire fuels a distorted breakcore soundtrack that surges in volume with your aggression. Survive the nightmare. Dance until you drown in the noise.

In One Night in Venice, your only goal is to shoot and survive waves of ruthless enemies who want nothing but your end. Keep your eyes sharp and your reflexes faster if they catch you, you'll have to start it all over AGAIN. Push yourself to beat your personal record with every run. But above all else: don't stop running.

Implementation Insights - Enemy

Key Learnings from Enemy movement Implementation

In One Night in Venice enemies kill you in a single hit The main challenge was creating enemies that posed a real threat without being frustrating.

Having them simply rush the player in a straight line felt cheap After brainstorming, I decided to make the enemies orbit around the player, gradually closing the distance. This dynamic allows players to "tame" the horde through smart movement.

implemented this behavior using mathematical calculations playing with sine waves (sin) to achieve that smooth orbiting and "blob-like" crowd behavior.

Additionally, I ensured that enemies always dynamically look at the player based on their relative position.

Technical Challenge

The key was to have a game that could be replayed. Adding unique elements to each playthrough enhances the player experience.

Progressive Difficulty Design

One of the key features of One Night in Venice was the roguelike power-up card system. Throughout each run, after collecting a certain number of gems, players could choose to boost stats like damage, bullet speed, or movement speed.

This progression was crucial because the game became scaled in difficulty not by making enemies stronger, but by drastically increasing spawn quantities.

Structurally, I created a system using Data Tables to link the card data directly to the corresponding gameplay variables and connected them to the UI.

I then implemented a shuffle algorithm to ensure that the card selection was completely randomized upon each level-up.

Implementation Insights - Enemy feedbacks

Hit stop and flash hit effect

Game feedback is absolutely vital; elements like audio cues, screen shake, and haptics drastically increase the richness of the player experience. My goal was to maximize this impact.

Beyond adding heavy hit sounds and enemy audio, I focused on implementing the classic retro "white flash" hit effect, inspired by games like Metal Slug and Cuphead. Technically, I achieved this in Unreal Engine by creating a custom white material flash that triggers dynamically.

Using Blueprints, I utilized a Timeline to handle the jitter and duration, swapping the enemy's material instantly whenever a bullet registers a hit.
