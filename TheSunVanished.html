The Sun Vanished

Survival Horror - LiDAR Navigation

Role: Solo Developer (Design, Tech, Level Design)

About the Project

In "The Sun Vanished" you play as an investigator trapped in complete darkness after a surreal event. The world around you has become an impenetrable void, and your only way to navigate is through a LiDAR scanner that shoots particles, revealing the shapes of walls, objects, and the environment around you.

This was a solo project developed in about 2 weeks. I handled everything from concept to implementation: the LiDAR navigation mechanic, the sensory AI of the invisible monster, the UI system, and the level design.

The goal is simple but tense: find the code to open a locked door and escape. But be careful, an invisible creature roams the darkness, and it can hear your every move.

Core Mechanics

LiDAR Scanner

The LiDAR scanner shoots particles into the darkness, revealing the shapes of walls, furniture, and environmental obstacles. This is your ONLY way to navigate and understand your surroundings.

Sensory AI

The invisible monster doesn't see — it HEARS. Every footstep, every interaction, every LiDAR shot creates noise. The creature uses a sound-based perception system to hunt you.

Investigation Goal

Your objective is to explore the environment, find clues, and discover a numeric code that unlocks the exit door. The code is procedurally placed to encourage exploration.

Implementation Insights - LiDAR Scanner

Designing the LiDAR Navigation

The core challenge was creating a navigation system that works in complete darkness. I didn't want to use a simple flashlight, I wanted something more unique that fits the "investigator" theme.

The LiDAR scanner uses rays that bounce off surfaces and return visual feedback to the player. Each shot reveals a portion of the environment, forcing players to scan strategically rather than holding a button.

I learned that sound design is critical for this mechanic. The ping of the scanner, the echo of particles hitting walls, and the ambient silence between scans all contribute to the tension.

The system was implemented using Unreal Engine's line traces and small sprites, optimized to run smoothly while providing enough visual information for navigation.

Implementation Insights - Sensory AI (Sound-Based)

Click to zoom - AI hearing system move to sound.

Building an Invisible Hunter

One of the most interesting challenges was creating an invisible enemy that feels fair yet terrifying. Since players can't see the monster, they need other ways to sense its presence.

The AI uses a sound-based perception system, it "hears" player actions: footsteps, LiDAR scans, door interactions, and object pickups. Each action generates a sound radius that attracts the creature.

I implemented distance-based audio cues for the monster's movement. When it's far, you hear distant growls. When it's close, you hear footsteps and breathing. This gives players feedback without breaking the invisibility concept.

The AI includes patrol, investigation, and chase states, with the monster searching the last known sound location before returning to patrol.

Implementation Insights - Level Design & Pacing

Designing Tension Through Environment

With only 2 weeks of development, I had to focus on smart level design rather than large environments. The level is relatively small but uses non-linear exploration to create replayability.

The code required to open the exit door is hidden across multiple environmental clues. Players need to explore, use the LiDAR, and piece together information while avoiding the monster.

I placed safe zones and danger corridors to control the pacing. Some areas are safer for LiDAR scanning, others force players to move quickly and risk making noise.

The final game loop: Scan → Navigate → Find Clue → Avoid Monster → Input Code → Escape. This created a tight, tense experience despite the short development time.
