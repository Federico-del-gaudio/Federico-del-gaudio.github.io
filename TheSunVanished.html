The Sun Vanished

Survival Horror - LiDAR Navigation

Role: Solo Developer (Design, Tech, Level Design)

About the Project

In "The Sun Vanished" you play as an investigator trapped in complete darkness after a surreal event. Your only way to navigate is through a LiDAR scanner that reveals the shapes of walls, objects, and the environment around you.

A solo project developed in about 2 weeks. I handled everything: the LiDAR navigation mechanic, the sensory AI of the invisible monster, the UI system, and the level design.

Your goal: find the code to open a locked door and escape. But an invisible creature roams the darkness, and it can hear your every move.

The Iterative Process

"The First Prototype"

LiDAR · Darkness · Sensory AI

With only 2 weeks to develop a complete horror experience, I had to make quick decisions. The initial concept was a standard flashlight horror game, but I wanted something more unique and fitting for an "investigator" theme.

After researching, I landed on LiDAR technology, a scanning system that reveals environment geometry through particle traces. The core idea was simple: you can't see anything, but you can scan to understand your surroundings.

"The Challenge"

The initial version had no enemy, so I created an AI, keeping in mind the main challenge: the player couldn't see it, and the map was completely black. As a result, I gave the AI sound-based perception only.

The monster's behavior evolved through playtesting. Initially it was too aggressive, then too passive. I found the sweet spot when the creature patrols, investigates sounds, and gives audio warnings before attacking.

"Speaking to the player"

The problem was that a single-color lidar combined with a black map failed to make key gameplay objects stand out.

The player needed to pick up or spot objects essential for progressing through the level, but relying on shape alone to make them stand out wasn't enough.

I changed the color of the lidar beam whenever it passed over a key object, letting the player know that it is an important object.

Implementation Insights - LiDAR Scanner

Designing the LiDAR Navigation

The core challenge was creating a navigation system in complete darkness. I didn't want a simple flashlight, I wanted something unique that fits the "investigator" theme.

The LiDAR scanner uses rays that bounce off surfaces and return visual feedback. Each shot reveals a portion of the environment, forcing players to scan strategically rather than holding a button.

Sound design is critical for this mechanic. The ping of the scanner, the echo of particles hitting walls, and the ambient silence between scans all contribute to the tension.

Implemented using Unreal Engine's line traces and small sprites, optimized to run smoothly while providing enough visual information for navigation.

Implementation Insights - Sensory AI

AI hearing system - move to sound source

Building an Invisible Hunter

One of the most interesting challenges was creating an invisible enemy that feels fair yet terrifying. Since players can't see the monster, they need other ways to sense its presence.

The AI uses a sound-based perception system, it "hears" player actions: footsteps, LiDAR scans, door interactions, and object pickups. Each action generates a sound radius that attracts the creature.

Distance-based audio cues for the monster's movement. Far = distant growls. Close = footsteps and breathing. This gives players feedback without breaking invisibility.

The AI includes patrol, investigation, and chase states, with the monster searching the last known sound location before returning to patrol.

Implementation Insights - Level Design

Designing Tension Through Environment

With only 2 weeks, I focused on smart level design rather than large environments. The level is relatively small but uses non-linear exploration to create replayability.

The code required to open the exit door is hidden across multiple environmental clues. Players need to explore, use the LiDAR, and piece together information while avoiding the monster.

Safe zones and danger corridors control the pacing. Some areas are safer for LiDAR scanning, others force players to move quickly and risk making noise.

Final game loop: Scan → Navigate → Find Clue → Avoid Monster → Input Code → Escape.

Level raw layout - non-linear exploration

Closing Thoughts

The Sun Vanished was a solo development sprint that taught me the value of focused scope and smart systems design. In just 2 weeks, I had to prioritize what mattered most: the core navigation loop and the invisible threat.

The LiDAR mechanic was the biggest technical challenge. Creating a system that reveals environment geometry in darkness while maintaining performance required careful optimization. I'm proud of the result, it creates a unique tension that standard flashlights can't match.

This project also reinforced the importance of sound design in horror. With an invisible enemy, audio becomes the primary feedback system. Every footstep, every scan ping, every distant growl matters.

For future occasions, I will give more serious consideration to the idea of ​​managing the LiDAR via the Niagara system.
