The Last Coin

Survival Horror

Role: Technical Designer

About the Project

In the suffocating dark of a gambling nightmare, you are forced to face your addiction. Wander an endless corridor of despair and challenge your Destiny at the cursed Slot Machine. Ransack the Panic Room in a desperate hunt for coins, but beware the slow fraying of your Sanity.

For this project, my primary focus was engine-side implementation. I was responsible for integrating UI/UX systems, gameplay mechanics, and providing audio support to programmers throughout the in-engine development process.

Implementation Insights - Horror Systems

Sanity system showing visual distortion effects as player sanity decreases.

Key Learnings from Horror Implementation

During the development of The Last Coin, I learned that psychological horror requires careful pacing and environmental storytelling. The atmosphere needed to build tension gradually while maintaining player engagement.

The most significant insight came from implementing the Sanity system. I discovered that subtle audio and visual distortions were more effective than overt scary moments. Players responded better to psychological unease than jump scares.

Another crucial learning was the balance between player agency and narrative control. The gambling mechanics needed to feel random enough to create tension, but controlled enough to maintain game balance and narrative progression.

Finally, I learned the importance of audio layering in horror. Subtle ambient sounds, carefully timed musical cues, and spatial audio effects created a much more immersive and terrifying experience than visual effects alone.

QTE System for in-game events - Flashlight failure mechanics.

QTE System Implementation Insights

The implementation of QTEs was essential to trigger intense moments of anxiety for the player. In this Blueprint, the system forces the player's flashlight to suddenly fail; the player must then repeatedly mash the "E" key to turn it back on.

The design philosophy of this game doesn't rely on cheap jump scares; instead, it focuses on building psychological tension and making the player feel deeply vulnerable through environmental sounds and unpredictable systemic events.

I implemented the QTE system using Unreal Engine's Blueprint scripting, creating a reusable component that could be attached to various interactive objects. The system tracks button press frequency, success thresholds, and provides visual/audio feedback based on player performance.

A key technical challenge was ensuring the QTE timing felt fair while maintaining tension. After multiple playtests, I adjusted the timing windows and input buffering to create a sweet spot between challenge and frustration.
